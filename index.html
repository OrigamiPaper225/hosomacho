Shoulder history: stop anything that hurts. Use pain-free range and light loads. No overhead pressing, dips, upright rows or deep flyes for now. The dead hang puts the arm fully overhead: keep feet on a box at first and drop it if the shoulder aches. If pain, weakness or limited motion remains, get a physio to adjust exercises. This is a training plan, not rehab.
Warm-up · before every session (5–8 min)
  • 5 min easy bike or treadmill walk.
  • 1–2 light ramp-up sets of the first exercise. These do not count as work sets.
  • Band external rotation, very light, 1–2 × 12. Only if pain-free.
Stretches · after every session (4 min)
  • Chest doorway stretch, elbow below shoulder — 2 × 20–30 s. Gentle; skip if the shoulder pinches.
  • Cross-body shoulder stretch — 2 × 20–30 s per side, only if comfortable.
  • Lat stretch on a rack, hips back — 2 × 20–30 s per side.
  • Hamstring stretch — 2 × 20–30 s per side.
  • Hip-flexor stretch (half kneel) — 2 × 20–30 s per side.

Never force an end range on the injured shoulder.

How to progress
  • Weeks 1–2: 2 sets of everything, light weight, learn the movement.
  • Effort: end each set with 2–3 good reps left. No need to hit failure.
  • Add weight when every set reaches the top of the rep range with clean form. Use the smallest jump.
  • Rest: 90–150 s for presses, rows, pulldowns. 60–90 s for curls, triceps, core.
  • Short on time: pair a curl with a pressdown, or an arm move with core.
  • Missed a day: skip it. Don't stack it onto the next week.
Nut allergy: check every label. Recipes contain no nuts, but protein powder, doubanjiang, sauces and restaurant food can contain nuts or be made alongside them. Read the allergen statement and "may contain" line every time you buy.

Daily targets + supplements
  • Protein: 100–120 g a day (about 1.8–2.1 g per kg). The daily plan lands there.
  • Calories: start near 2,000 a day. This is an estimate, not a measured number.
  • Adjust: weigh in most mornings and compare weekly averages. If weight drops 2 weeks in a row, add about 150–200 kcal a day, mostly as rice. If waist grows fast, pull back a little.
  • Rice: about 1 cup cooked per meal. White rice is fine.
  • Creatine monohydrate: 3 g a day, every day, with any meal. No loading phase. Expect 1–2 lb of water weight early. Ask a doctor first if you have kidney problems.
  • Protein powder: optional, for convenience. Whey isolate if dairy sits well, or soy isolate. Start with half a scoop.
  • Start supplements one at a time, a week apart, so you can tell if one upsets your gut.
Gut: needing to go after every meal

The gastrocolic reflex makes the colon move after eating. Larger, fattier meals trigger it more strongly. If it happens after every meal, see a primary-care doctor; a functional gut disorder can cause this.

  • Keep meals moderate in size. Go light on oil.
  • Mapo tofu: start with 1 tsp doubanjiang and skip chili oil.
  • Log meals, stool type, urgency, coffee and supplements for 1–2 weeks to bring to the doctor.
  • Cut only foods your log shows as triggers. No broad elimination diet.

See a doctor promptly for blood or black stool, severe belly pain, signs of dehydration, diarrhea lasting more than 2 days, or weight loss you can't explain.
